01|feed|Every year the Consumer Product Safety Commission publishes hundreds of recall notices. This is the live feed, right now. Four hundred and thirty four notices so far in twenty twenty six. Forty two percent of them involve products made for children. Three hundred and seventeen of them have the word death in the title.
02|gap|Almost nobody finds out, and it is not because people do not care. A recall notice is a paragraph written by a lawyer, describing packaging. Across all four hundred and thirty four, three percent carry a barcode you could look your purchase up by. None carry a model number. There is nothing to join on.
03|pair|Here is what matching actually looks like. The notice describes finger light toys, fifty pieces in a box, white, blue, red and green. The receipt says L E D projecting finger lights, party favors, fifty pieces. Those are the same object, and they share almost no words. Reading that is the one part of this problem a language model is better at than a rule.
04|run|So that is the only thing Pullback lets the model do. Watch a real run. It pulls the candidate notices, reads one, and says what it thinks and why. What comes back is not its own opinion. It is a verdict computed outside it, from the purchase date against the notice's sold window, the price against the price band, and the retailer. Four checks passed. The case opens and the claim gets written, citing the recall number.
05|gate|And then it stops. Sending a message to a company in someone's name is not the agent's call to make, so it is the one tool that can never be trusted away. The claim is written, the evidence is attached, and it waits for a person.
06|console|That person sees this. Not a dashboard, a decision queue, and its healthy state is empty because the agent closed everything it could close alone. Two things need a human today. Open one and the product has to prove it was not guessing: every check, in order, with the actual values it compared. The notice itself is one click away on c p s c dot gov, so nothing here has to be taken on trust.
07|veto|Now the part that matters. Approve a claim for something the household does not own, and order the agent to send it anyway. It refuses. But a model that politely declines has proved nothing, because a guardrail only ever reached by well behaved callers is untested. So this test calls the tool directly, the way a misbehaving model would, with the household's approval already granted. A hook cancels it before it runs. Approval is permission to send a claim that passed. It is never permission to skip the checking.
08|mutation|Here is that check failing on purpose. Delete the hook, and a real claim goes out to a real company for a product nobody owns, and the suite goes red. Put it back, and eight hundred and twenty nine tests pass.
09|unattended|None of this needs anyone watching. EventBridge Scheduler runs a Lambda that carries the whole agent, every day. Here is the log from a run nobody triggered, reading identity in production. And this is the number that matters on the second run: zero cases created, seven updated. The case id is a hash of the household, the purchase and the notice, so a rerun can never file the same claim twice.
10|breadth|It is not only toys. The same engine reads vehicle recalls and food enforcement reports, and they do not describe identity the same way at all. This household's car matches four real N H T S A campaigns, decided on the model, not on any text. Food recalls carry no price and no sold window ever, only lot codes, so those cases are settled on the code or not at all.
11|label|Which leaves the hardest question this product asks. Sometimes the only thing that would settle a case is a code stamped on the object itself, and nobody knows it by heart. So the agent asks for a photograph, and reads it. It read this barcode correctly and the case became a match. And when the photograph cannot be read, it says so and asks for a better one, instead of inventing a code. That matters more than the successful read, because an invented lot code is a false claim against a real company.
12|reply|When the company writes back, the agent reads the reply and moves the case to where it now actually stands. A refund confirmed resolves it. A vague acknowledgement with a ticket number does not, because a product that marks itself successful on an autoreply is worse than useless.
13|close|Pullback is for a parent with a toddler and a purchase history, who has never returned a registration card and does not want another app to check. The gap between a product being recalled and it leaving the house is where children get hurt. Built with the Strands Agents SDK, on A W S.
