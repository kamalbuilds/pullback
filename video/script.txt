Forty two percent of them involve products made for children. Three hundred and seventeen of them have the word death in the title.
02|gap|Almost nobody finds out. Not because people do not care, but because a recall notice is a paragraph written by a lawyer, describing packaging. Across all four hundred and thirty four, three percent carry a barcode you could look your purchase up by. None carry a model number. There is nothing to join on.
03|pair|Here is what matching actually looks like. The notice describes finger light toys, fifty pieces in a box, white, blue, red and green. The receipt says L E D projecting finger lights, party favors, fifty pieces. Those are the same object, and they share almost no words. Reading that is the one part of this problem a language model is genuinely better at than a rule.
04|run|So that is the only thing Pullback lets the model do. Watch a real run. It pulls the candidate notices, then it reads one, and it says what it thinks and why. What comes back is not its own opinion. It is a verdict computed outside it, from the purchase date against the notice's sold window, the price against the notice's price band, and the retailer. Four checks passed. The case opens, the claim gets written citing the recall number, and it is filed with the address printed on the notice.
05|console|This is the console. It is not a dashboard, it is a decision queue, and its healthy state is empty, because the agent closed everything it could close on its own. Two things need a person today. Everything on this screen was read out of the live case table three seconds ago.
06|case|Open one and the product has to prove it was not guessing. Every check, in the order it ran, with the actual values it compared. Bought April twelfth, inside a sold window that runs December twenty twenty five to July twenty twenty six. Twelve ninety nine, inside a band of ten forty to fifteen sixty. And the notice itself is one click away on c p s c dot gov, so you can check that nothing here was invented.
07|veto|Now the part that matters. Ask the agent to file a claim on something the household does not own, and order it to ignore the verdict. It refuses. But a model that politely declines has not proved anything, because a guardrail only reached by well behaved callers is untested. So this test calls the tool directly, the way a misbehaving model would. A hook cancels it before it runs.
08|mutation|And here is that check failing on purpose. Delete the hook, and a real claim goes out to a real company for a product nobody owns, and the suite goes red. Put it back, and everything passes. That is the difference between an agent asked to behave and one that cannot misbehave.
09|unattended|None of this needs anyone watching. EventBridge Scheduler runs it daily against a Lambda that carries the whole agent. Here is the log from a run nobody triggered, reading identity in production. And this is the number that matters on a second run: zero cases created, seven updated. The case id is a hash of the household, the purchase and the notice, so a rerun can never file the same claim twice.
10|evidence|Every decision leaves something a person can audit months from now, in object storage: the case, the claim, and every check that ran with the values it compared.
